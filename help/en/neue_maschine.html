New machine

New machine … builds a ready-made machine in a new document: an assembly with joints, axes with names and characteristics, spindles, tool and workpiece mounts. Afterwards Edit machine opens, where everything can be looked at and changed.

Types

You can choose a lathe with Y axis and turret, a 3-axis mill and three 5-axis mills (table/table, head/head, head/table). For every type you enter the main dimensions yourself – for the 5-axis mills also how far they swivel.

The table of the built 3-axis mill is empty. Whatever stands on your table (vice, clamps) you build as a body into the assembly and fix it to the table with a fixed joint – the collision check (“Check on machine”) then takes it into account. The example mill of the checks has two clamps for that (bar, bolt, nut).

The dimensions of the lathe

  • Name – for machine and document. Empty: “Lathe with Y axis”.
  • Bed slant – how steeply the bed rises towards the back: 0° is a flat bed, 30°, 45° or 60° are common. X travels on the bed, so it is slanted the same way.
  • Y inclined by – 0° means the Y slide moves at right angles to X. If the Y slide of your machine is built at an angle to the X slide, enter the angle here. The addon then adds the inclined axis right away: for a Y in the program both slides move, the way the control converts (see the help “Inclined axes”).
  • X as – Diameter (Ø) or Radius: how your control shows X and expects it in the program. On almost every lathe it is the diameter, so that is preselected. The machine remembers it (at X1 in “Edit machine” the tick “counts in diameter (Ø)”): all windows then show X doubled, with “Ø” in front, and the add-on's post processor writes X that way into the program. Calculations always use the radius.
  • Travel X, Y, Z – from … to, counted as on the machine: up to the centre of the VDI mount in working position, at its face (the reference point of the turret). X from the spindle axis, in diameter or as a radius – as chosen under “X as”; switching converts the fields. X 0 means the centre of the mount is on the spindle axis, a drill drills into the centre of the part there; negative is past the centre. Z from the spindle nose, along the spindle axis. Y from the spindle centre; 0 lies in between. For “from” the minus is already in the field – you type only the number (except for Z: both ends count from the spindle nose). These become the limits of the joints. The machine is built at X Ø 550 (radius 275) and Z 220 mm; if that lies outside your travels, it moves inside.
  • Turret stations – the addon distributes this many stations in a circle (P1 … Pn), each a VDI mount on the tool drive (S3). The turret carries no fixed holders: how a tool stands is up to its holder in the tool manager.
  • Turret – where the mounts sit. VDI in the face (axial): the holders go into the disc along Z – a straight holder drills along Z, an angled one (“VDI30 driven radial”) stands radial to the spindle axis. VDI on the circumference (radial, star turret): the holders go in radially – a straight holder stands radial to the spindle axis, an angle head mills along Z, towards the chuck. “All-round roughing” needs a radial tool; the yellow sentence in the 4-axis wizard says which holder fits.
  • Disc Ø – the diameter of the turret disc (240 … 600 mm). Mounts in the face lie 40 mm inside the edge, those on the circumference on the edge.
  • VDI size – the shank Ø of the holders (VDI 20 … 60), the size of the ring around each mount. Choose the holders to match in the tool manager.
  • Maximum speed – of the main spindle and of the driven tools in the turret (tool drive); the second is often lower. “Plan roughing values” takes it from the machine.
  • Spindles – the numbers of the main spindle (default 1) and of the driven tools (default 3). Each gets an S and a C with its number: the main spindle S1 (turns the part) and C1 (positions it, the rotary axis for milling), the tool drive S3 (turns the tool) and C3 (orients it). If your control calls them e.g. S4/C4 and S1/C1, enter 4 and 1 – then the postprocessor writes “SPOS[4]=0”, “C4=…” and “M1=3 S1=…”.

The dimensions of the 3-axis mill

  • Name – for machine and document. Empty: “3-axis mill”.
  • Travel X, Y, Z – from … to, counted from the position in which the machine is built; 0 lies in between. “from” goes negative – the minus is already in the field, you type only the number. These become the limits of the joints – “Check on machine” works with them.
  • Maximum speed – of spindle S1.

The dimensions of the 5-axis mills

Name, travels and maximum speed as for the 3-axis mill, plus one row per swivel axis:

  • Swivel A (table/table), Swivel B (head/table), A and B (head/head) – from … to in degrees, counted from the home orientation: there the tool stands vertically above the table. As with the travels, the minus is already in the field. These become the limits of the joints – “Swivel plane (3+2)” only uses orientations within them, “Check on machine” reports what would have to go beyond.
  • The rotary table C (table/table, head/table) turns endlessly.

When you switch the type, its example values are back in the fields.

The bodies are rough, the way a machine is built in the addon. Whatever does not fit – the size of a slide, the position of the turret – you change afterwards in the assembly.

The joints are hidden – their markers (white discs with axes) covered the machine. To show them: select “Joints” under the assembly in the tree and press the space bar.

Axis names as on your control

The example machines call the axes X1, Y1, Z1, T and the spindles S1/C1 and S3/C3 (on the lathe you enter the spindle numbers right above). If your control names them differently, open “Edit machine” after building, click the row and change the NC name. The program then calls the axes that way.
