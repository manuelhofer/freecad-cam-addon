Cutting data

One row per use case – that is, per way the tool works. You enter the four values found in the manufacturer's catalogue; the addon calculates spindle speed, feed rate and metal removal rate from them (grey).

What you enter

  • ae – the radial depth of cut in mm: how wide the cutter engages the material. In a full slot, ae is the whole diameter.
  • ap – the axial depth of cut in mm, along the tool axis: how much of the flute length is working.
  • vc – the cutting speed in m/min: how fast the edge moves through the material.
  • fz – the feed per tooth in mm: how thick a chip each tooth takes. For a drill, this column shows f, the feed per revolution.

ae and ap in mm or in % of D: the choice at the right above the table (“ae, ap: mm” / “ae, ap: % of D”) shows and takes both as a percentage of the diameter if you like – 10 % at Ø 12 is 1.2 mm, ap 25 mm is 208 %. They are always saved in mm; the choice applies to all tools and is kept for next time. Without a diameter they are shown in mm.

What the addon calculates

  • n (rpm) = vc · 1000 / (π · D)
  • vf (mm/min) = n · z · fz
  • Q (cm³/min) = ae · ap · vf / 1000 – that much material is gone per minute. For drilling: π · D² / 4 · vf / 1000.

Example: end mill Ø 12, 3 flutes, vc 120 m/min, fz 0.05 mm → n = 3183 rpm, vf = 477 mm/min. As a full slot (ae 12, ap 3) that is 17.2 cm³/min.

The use cases

+ Use case offers the usual ones and prefills ae and ap from the diameter. vc and fz stay empty – they are in the catalogue.

  • Full slot – ae = D, ap = D/2.
  • Roughing – pockets and faces the conventional way: ae = D/2, ap = D/2.
  • Dynamic roughing – narrow and deep: ae = 10 % of D, ap = the flute length (at most 2 × D). Trochoidal, HPC, “adaptive” – all the same idea. Why this is often much better is explained under Compare strategies.
  • Finishing – ae = 2 % of D, ap = the flute length.
  • Own use case – everything free. Double-click a row's name to change it.

+ Use case → Copy the selected row puts a copy directly below the selected row, with a number in its name: “Dynamic roughing 2”. This way you try a variant – say ae 1.8 instead of 1.2 – and put both side by side under Compare strategies. A new row also gets a number whenever its name already exists, so the rows stay distinguishable in the comparison and in the job.

For all materials – or for one

The first column of the table is the material the row applies to – selectable in every row; the selection shows what is known about the material as a tooltip. All materials applies to every material without a row of its own. A new row (+ Use case, the copy, the planner) gets the material of the selected row – without a selected row “All materials”; afterwards you change it in the column. If a material has rows of its own, only they apply to it; delete its last one and the rows for all materials apply again. In the job and in the assistants, what applies to the stock's material applies. “Materials…” at the top opens the material list with search, all data and your own materials.

Engagement and chip thickness

Below the table there is a picture of the selected row: on the left, headed “ae – radial depth of cut”, from above, the cutter with the material and, in red, the arc over which each tooth is in the material; on the right, headed “ap – axial depth of cut”, from the side, the flutes and, in red, the part that works at this ap. Next to it the same values in words, one line per value – ae in mm and % of D, ap in mm, as a multiple of D and in % of the flutes:

  • Engagement – the angle of that arc. Full slot: 180°, the tooth is in the material for half a revolution and gets hot. At ae = 10 % of D it is 37°: the tooth cuts briefly and cools down for the rest of the revolution.
  • ae in % of D and ap in × D – the way many catalogues state them; plus how much of the flutes is working.
  • Largest chip thickness – with ae below D/2 the chip gets thinner than fz: h max = fz · sin(engagement). At 10 % of D it is only 60 % as thick. If it is too thin, the edge rubs instead of cutting – it gets hot and wears without removing material.

Chip thinning: the fz values in catalogues usually apply from ae = D/2. Enter the chip thickness you want – usually exactly that fz – and the addon calculates the fz that gives it at the narrow engagement. Example Ø 12, ae 1.2: wanted 0.09 mm → fz 0.15 mm.

Where the values come from

From the manufacturer's catalogue, under the material's ISO group (the coloured box). For orientation only, for coated solid carbide end mills when roughing:
