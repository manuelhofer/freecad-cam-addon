Getting started – from the part to the program

Six steps. Each has its button in the CAM-Addon toolbar (it hangs in the Assembly and the CAM workbench) and in the “CAM-Addon” menu. You set up the machine and the tools once (steps 1 and 2); for every further part, steps 3 to 6 remain. In every window the ? button at the right of a heading opens the help on what you are looking at.

1 Build the machine

New machine … builds a fully set-up machine in a new document – a lathe with Y axis and turret, a 3-axis or a 5-axis mill, with your dimensions (travels, speed, turret stations, bed inclination). Save the document: you store the machine once and load it for every job. You turn an assembly of your own into a machine with Edit machine – axes, spindles, tool and workpiece mounts; Move machine shows whether it moves correctly. All your machines are listed in Machines ….
More: Machines, New machine, Axes and operating modes, Mounts, Move machine.

2 Enter the tools

Tool management: every tool with its type and dimensions, its length from the spindle nose, its holder and its cutting data per material and application – vc and fz from the catalogue, speed and feed are calculated by the addon. Length and holder are needed later by the check on the machine, the cutting data by the jobs. In Magazines you set, per machine, under which number a tool sits there.
More: Tools, Holders, Magazines, Cutting data, Materials.

3 Part and machining

A part from round bar: click the flat face that should sit at the front of the bar (usually the end face), then 4-axis machining – the assistant puts the part into the bar, sets up the CAM job and asks “What do you want to do?”: all-round roughing and finishing, selected faces, flats, cross holes, slots, deburring – with cutter, application and stepdown from the tool management.
A part in a block on a mill: click a face of the part, then Machining (milling) – the assistant creates job and stock, shows at the top how much has to go and how long the cutter needs for it at least (the target), below it the strategies that fit the selection; where two solve the same task, the faster one gets the tick.
On a 5-axis mill an inclined face: click it, then Swivel plane (3+2) … – the plane becomes a job of its own in which the assistant mills as from above; one program for the base job and its planes (Siemens with CYCLE800).
A FreeCAD CAM job (pocket, profile, drilling …): create it as usual, then Cutting data into job – speed and feed of its tool controllers come from the tool management, matching the material of the stock.
More: 4-axis machining, Machining (milling), Swivel plane (3+2), Cutting data.

4 Tool loading

Select the job, then Tool loading: which tool of the job sits in which turret station – every job has its own (a base job shares it with its swivelled planes); in the program the number is the station (T5 in P5). If the machine has no turret, skip this step.
More: Tool loading.

5 Check on machine

Select the job, then Check on machine: do the axes reach, does anything collide, do speed and feed still match the tool management? The verdicts at the top, the details below – a click moves the machine to the spot. Playback runs the path on the machine; at the end the bar shows in colours against the finished part: green the allowance, red where too much remained, blue where something is missing in the part.
More: Check on machine.

6 Write the program

Select the job, then Write program … in the toolbar: the addon's postprocessor writes all operations with the commands of your control (LinuxCNC, Siemens 840D, Fanuc, Haas, Mach3/Mach4, Heidenhain iTNC 530 and TNC 640) – on a lathe with X as diameter, C axis on and off and the command for the driven tool; rotary axes per DIN 66217. More: Write program. FreeCAD's own Post Process command in the CAM menu still works.
