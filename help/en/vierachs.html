4-axis machining

A part is milled out of a round bar while the bar turns – on a mill with a rotary axis (A or B) just as on a lathe with a C axis and driven tools. The wizard gets you there in two steps: placing the part in the bar and then “What do you want to do?” – so far All-round roughing. Choosing faces and finishing come in the next stages.

How it works

  1. Open the document with the part and, in the 3D view, click the flat face that should sit at the front of the bar – usually the end face.
  2. In the “CAM-Addon” toolbar, click 4-axis machining. The part moves into a see-through bar and turns once about the axis.
  3. Enter the bar diameter, say 80. The line at the bottom says in green how much material is left all round at least – or in red which diameter the part would need.
  4. Next – step 2: What do you want to do? “All-round roughing” is ticked; below are cutter, use case and values from the tool manager and, in grey, how many layers it takes – say “→ 5 layers (Ø 80.0 mm → Ø 60.6 mm)”.
  5. Create – the CAM job with the part in the bar, the tool controller and the operation “All-round roughing T1” appear; its path runs round the part in layers. The first Ctrl+Z undoes controller and operation, the second job and bar. Cancel leaves nothing behind.

Back returns to the stock. Without the tick at “All-round roughing”, “Create” only creates job and bar.

Clicking another face while the window is open puts the part into the bar with that face at the front.

How the part sits in the bar

  • Front is the free end of the bar, away from the chuck. The chosen face sits there, with the bar sticking out by the face allowance. The zero along the axis lies in this face, the part behind it – like Z0 on a lathe.
  • Centre of the round face – if the outer edge of the face is a circle, its centre sits on the bar axis (the centroid for a face with corners). That way a shaft stays round even when a cam sticks out.
  • Whole part as central as possible – the part is placed so that it fits the thinnest bar.
  • Next to both you see the diameter the bar needs for it. The round face is preselected if the part fits the bar that way, otherwise the whole part.
  • Rotation turns the part in the bar about the axis and so sets where 0° of the rotary axis is.

The bar

Behind the part come the parting width (room for parting off or sawing) and the clamping length (how far the bar sits in the chuck). Together they give the length of the bar, shown in grey next to the fields.

Everything can be set, and empty fields use their grey suggestion. What you enter becomes next time's suggestion. The bar diameter depends on the part. Its suggestion is the next 5 mm step (1/8" in inch) that leaves at least 1 mm all round.

The rotary axis

The axis the bar turns about decides how it lies in the job.

  • With a machine: if a machine is open (“Edit machine”), its rotary axis is listed first and preselected – such as “Machine “Lathe”: C – bar along Z”. The bar then lies in the job the way it is clamped in that machine: on a lathe along the spindle, the front away from the chuck. “Check on machine” shows it exactly like that.
  • Without a machine you assign the letter to the bar yourself: A – the bar lies along X, the tool comes from above (a mill with rotary axis A); B – along Y, the tool from above; C – along Z, the tool from X, like a lathe with a C axis.

The bar axis runs through the job's origin. That way FreeCAD lays later paths correctly around the part by itself.

Step 2: all-round roughing

The bar is removed all round down to the finishing allowance – in layers, with a cutter pointing at the axis from outside while the rotary axis turns.

  • Material – which one the cutting data are for: the stock's if it has one, otherwise the one used last.
  • Cutter – from the tool manager: end, torus, ball, slot or face mill with cutting data for the material. Preselected is the one used last, otherwise the first end mill. If there is none, a red line says so; Tool manager … opens it – once you save there, it is available.
  • Use case – “Roughing” preselected. It gives spindle speed and feed (grey below) and the suggestions for the fields.
  • Step-down per layer – how deep each layer cuts radially (the use case's ap). Feed per revolution – how far the spiral advances along the bar per revolution (the use case's ae, at most the diameter). Finishing allowance – what stays all round (0.3 mm suggested).

The path: each layer is a spiral from the front – the cutter fully in front of the bar – to just before the chuck; the cutter's edge stays 2 mm in front of the clamping face. Where the part is higher than the layer, the tip follows the part plus allowance; it never comes closer to the axis than the cutter radius. Between layers it goes out radially and back to the front in rapid. It is calculated against the whole part, meshed to 0.02 mm: the error goes into the allowance, never into the part.

In the program – please read before the first run:

  • X is the radius (Z with A and B), not the diameter. On a lathe the program header must switch to radius (Siemens DIAMOF); otherwise the tool would go to half the distance from the axis.
  • The feed is given after G93 (1 ÷ time) – so the cutter runs at the use case's feed on the workpiece, however far it is from the axis. G94 follows. The post processor must write F in every block (its default) and output metric.
  • The rotary axis keeps counting (−3600° and more): it must run without modulo, otherwise it would not turn round several times.
  • At the start the tool moves out radially to the safety distance, then to the front of the bar and across to the centre (Y0 – not if the machine has no Y axis).

The values sit on the operation in the group “4-Achs” and can be changed there; the path is then recalculated.

What happens in the document

  • A new job “Part – 4 axes”. The part lies in the bar as the job's model. The original stays where it was and is hidden.
  • The job's stock is a cylinder with the bar's diameter and length. While the window is open it is see-through and cannot be clicked. Afterwards it looks like any stock in CAM.
  • With “All-round roughing” also a tool controller “T1 Roughing” with spindle speed and feed from the tool manager and the operation “All-round roughing T1”. The wizard removes the controller FreeCAD gives every new job.

What does not work (yet)

  • Milling only certain faces and finishing – that comes in the next stages. Until then “All-round roughing” takes the whole part.
  • Turning, facing the end and parting off are done by the machine – FreeCAD does not turn.
