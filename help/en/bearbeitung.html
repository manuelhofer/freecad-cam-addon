Machining (milling)

For a part that lies in a block on a mill – the tool axis is vertical. The button “Machining (milling)” opens the window; the way from the part to the operations has three steps; at the top it says which one you are in (“Step 1 of 3 – Setup”), at the bottom ← Back and Next → lead back and forth: the setup (machine, part, stock, origin), What comes off? (the faces and one tick per machining with its time), the settings of the ticked ones; in the last step Create stands at the bottom instead of “Next →”. “Create” (also “OK” of the task) works from every step – with what is suggested. The machinings: Face milling and Clearing for flat faces facing up, Drilling and Hole milling for cylindrical holes, Contour for walls, Tapping in core holes, Deburr on top edges, Spot drilling and Countersinking at holes, Rest material with the smaller cutter. “Create” creates all ticked ones. For a round stock with a rotary axis there is 4-axis machining.

1. Setup: machine, part and stock

  • Machine – at the very top: on which of your machines you mill the part, from the list in Machines … (the button next to it opens it). The one from last time is preselected; the job remembers the choice, and Check on machine then uses it without asking. If you machine the part elsewhere later, choose the other one here. If there is no machine in the list yet, it says “– no machine –”, and the assistant assumes a 3-axis mill. In step 2 the machine is shown first in the grey line.
  • Click the face you want to machine (before or in the window) – the face that should become flat, or a wall for the contour. This chooses the part, and the add-on creates the job with the stock: a block around the part with an allowance on top, at the sides and below (empty: 1 mm, like FreeCAD's job). The fields change the stock right away. “Cancel” takes everything back. Step 2 shows at the top in grey what applies here (“allowance 1 mm all round · origin: centre top”).
  • Lying on – which face of the part lies on the table: “as modelled”, or Click a face … and then a flat face in 3D – the part in the job turns so that it faces down; the model itself stays as it is. Without the button a click adds the face for machining, the part then never turns. “As modelled” turns it back.
  • X points – ↺ 90° and ↻ 90° turn the part in the job about Z (seen from above), so that X runs along the edge you want. Stock and origin follow both; the faces and all paths are computed in the new position.
  • Zero point – where X 0, Y 0, Z 0 of the program lie. Preselected is the centre top of the stock, afterwards the zero point you last created with. To choose: “as in the model”, or a point of the stock from the list – the 8 corners, the 12 edge middles, the centre top and bottom (left/right is X, front/back Y, bottom/top Z) –, shifted by X, Y, Z (negative too). Part and stock move so that the point lies at the origin; the axes in the 3D view show it, and “Check on the machine” counts from there.
  • When changing (double-click on the operation) position, stock and zero point stay as they are in the job.

2. What comes off? Faces and machinings

  • Without a choice the top of the part is milled flat – the highest flat faces. The sentence below the list names them.
  • A click on a face of the part adds it (another click, or a double-click in the list, removes it); green means: flat facing up – it is milled flat – or a wall (“wall, bottom 5 mm”) – the contour follows it; red means neither. The 3D view colours them so. “Top” selects the highest faces, “Clear selection” removes all.
  • The ticks of the blocks follow the selection: face milling and clearing as long as no face or a flat one is selected – and of the two only the faster (see “Time decides” below); contour as soon as a wall is among them (without a wall it cannot be ticked). Whoever sets or removes a tick keeps it.
  • What fits is on top: per machining that has to do with the selection, the tick with its result (time, and how much slower it would be) – the explanation is in the tooltip of the title. Its fields are in step 3 as long as it is ticked. Below, folded, Does not fit the selection with the number of strategies that do not work with this selection; a click opens the list: per strategy one line with its name and the sentence what to click in 3D for it (“Contour – No wall selected – click a vertical face in 3D.”). With nothing selected yet, the list stands open.

3. Settings – for example face milling

Per ticked machining its title, the explanation, cutter, application and fields – all preset; below, the same result line as in step 2. With nothing ticked, it says so there. When changing (double-click on the operation) the window opens right here.

  • Material – from the stock, otherwise the last used; the cutting data follow it. “Tool manager …” opens it; saving there makes the window read the cutters again.
  • Cutter – a cutter with a flat tip (flat, torus or face mill) from the tool manager with cutting data for the material; Application – “Facing” preselected; speed and feed in grey below. An end mill faces with a large ae at a small ap – the Ø 12 with ae 8.4 (0.7 D) and ap 1.2 (0.1 D): so it takes the millimetre above the top in one layer, almost four times as fast as with ae 1.5. With more than a few millimetres above the face, “Roughing” is faster (there clearing usually wins).
  • Stepdown per layer (ap), row spacing (ae, at most as wide as the flat part of the tip) and allowance – empty, the grey suggestions from the application apply.
  • climb milling only (lift, start again): off, the cutter runs the rows back and forth – fast, but every other row conventional. On, it runs every row climb milling, lifts at its end, rapids back and enters again at the start of the next; beside the row just milled vertically (only its strip ae is left there). If the tool controller is set to “Reverse” (M4), the direction turns with it. On the block 60 × 40 with a step: 2.25 instead of 1.87 min.
  • The path: rows along X or Y – the assistant computes both directions with their time and takes the faster one; the result line says how much slower the other would be –, back and forth, in layers from the top of the stock down to the face plus allowance; along the rows the cutter centre runs 0.6 × diameter past the face, at the sides the tip reaches 0.2 × diameter past the edge – so no burr remains. Between the rows it runs semicircles (G2/G3) where both rows are free there, otherwise across at depth. Before walls and steps that stand higher the row stops (calculated against the part without the face). Rows with no stock below them are dropped. Before a wall the cutter runs along it – back to the previous row and past the first and last row to the edge of the face – so that nothing remains between the rows and in the corners at the wall. Where a wall runs along the rows, one row runs right along it (before, up to one stepover remained there). Where a lower face of the same operation lies beside a face (the top of a boss, the floor around it), the upper one does not reach out to the edge of the stock there – the layer of the lower one starts at the stock and clears it anyway. Entry is straight down with the plunge feed in the air, a ramp in material. Leaving the stock the row runs at half feed – against the burr. What it looks like shows Check on machine: the block is removed while playing, at the end in colours against the finished part.
  • In grey below: “→ 3 layers, 30 rows, about 2 min” – the time with the feed, rapid 10 m/min and acceleration 1 m/s² (fixed defaults, see Finding the acceleration), stopping at every corner, so that strategies compare. In red stands why it does not work (no cutter with cutting data, no flat face facing up, the stock does not reach above the face).
  • Create adds the tool controller and the operation “Face milling T1” to the job – one undo step (job and stock a second one). The operation has FreeCAD's depths and heights: the start depth follows the stock, the final depth is the face plus allowance, safe and clearance height come from the job's setup sheet.

4. Clearing

  • For the same flat faces as face milling – and for pocket floors: when the walls of a pocket are selected, clearing takes their floor along (it is not ticked by itself for that – the contour stays the proposal; set the tick when the pocket is to be cleared).
  • Cutter and application as for face milling – “Roughing” preselected.
  • Stepdown per layer (ap – full, up to the cutting edge length), row spacing (ae, this far the rings step, at most the radius), allowance at walls (empty: 0.3 mm – stays on walls and islands, for the contour), allowance at the floor (empty: 0 – the face is finished) and the tick climb milling (with a clockwise spindle, M3: the material on the right of the direction of travel – clockwise around a boss, counter-clockwise in a pocket; without it conventional). If the tool controller is set to “Reverse” (M4), every path runs the other way round – climb milling stays climb milling.
  • The path, like an HSM path: rings at full stepdown and narrow row spacing, without turning, with arcs in the corners. On a top face circling from the outside inwards: the first ring lies beside the stock in the air, the cutter plunges there and enters tangentially; the rings follow the stock edge inwards until bosses and steps split them – then the rings around those. In a pocket from the inside outwards: once over a ramp all round (with the tool's plunge angle) into the middle, then the rings towards the wall. Every ring joins the previous one (a spiral); where that is not possible, the cutter plunges only where it is already free, otherwise it enters sideways from the free area – the ramp in material exists only where nothing is free. On leaving the stock at half feed.
  • The window computes three variants and takes the fastest: “rohteil” (the rings around what still stands: from the stock edge and from the islands, each ae further, equally spaced everywhere – the rectangle bites into the boss, the rings around it come last), “morph” (the first ring is a rectangle, then each gets rounder until the last one circles the boss exactly – one spiral without lifting; the rings are spaced so that the cutter nowhere takes more material than in a straight cut at the row spacing: closer in the corners, wider at the boss; only when the boss stands roughly in the middle) and “inseln” (rings only around the walls and islands; in a pocket the only one). In the operation's properties Gerechnet lists each with its time; Variante fixes one.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 layer, 15 rings, about 3 min”. Red says why it does not work (no flat floor, the cutter does not fit in, nothing stands above the face).
  • The operation is called “Räumen T1”; start depth, safe height and clearance height as for face milling, the final depth is the lowest face plus the allowance at the floor.
  • Then finish the walls – at the walls around the cleared faces (bosses, islands, steps) the allowance remains; with the tick the same cutter then runs along them in the allowance, with the “Finishing” application if it has one: an operation of its own, “Finish walls T1” (a contour with width = allowance, only the pass along the wall). The result line says so: “… – then finish the walls (1)”.
  • Measuring stop before finishing – only with the tick above: between clearing and finishing the program stops – Z moves above the stock, the spindle stops (M5, M0); measure, then start, the spindle runs up again with the finishing speed (M3). An operation of its own, “Measuring stop” (FreeCAD's “Custom”). A subroutine of the control such as Siemens “F_HOME” is written only by the add-on's own post processor (in progress); until then the program carries the comment (MESSSTOPP).

Time decides

Face milling and clearing solve the same task on a flat face. So the window computes both as soon as one is ticked, with the same time model (feed of the application, rapid 10 m/min, 1 m/s², stopping at corners) and ticks the faster; both lines say by how much: “… – the fastest; clearing would be 37 % slower” and “… – 37 % slower than face milling”. On the 60 × 40 block with a step face milling wins, on the 50 × 50 with a boss in the middle clearing does (3 instead of 5 minutes), on a large plate likewise. Whoever sets a tick keeps it – the lines still show the comparison. For a pocket the fastest sequence is: clearing (its floor, the walls with allowance) and then the contour with only the allowance – the contour alone clears every offset with its own ramp and takes several times as long. With the top face and a pocket wall selected, the window computes both sequences: face milling of the top and clearing only the pocket floor – or clearing everything. The faster one gets the ticks, the lines say by how much (“… – everything, the fastest sequence; face milling plus clearing the floors would be 13 % slower”). When clearing takes the floor of a pocket whose wall the contour runs, the window enters the clearing allowance as “material beside the wall” at the contour (as long as the field is empty), and the contour says so: “… – only the allowance at the walls: clearing clears the pocket”. When face milling (or clearing) mills the floors in front of all walls of the contour – a step, a boss –, the contour takes only the rest it leaves at the wall (with face milling at most one stepover, with clearing its allowance): “… – only the rest at the walls: face milling mills the floor in front”, “… – only the allowance at the walls: clearing clears the floor in front”. At a slot the window also computes the slot – on its floor against clearing, at its walls against the contour.

The target

Above the strategies, in grey, it says how much has to go and how long the cutter of clearing (otherwise of face milling, of the contour) needs for it at least with its values – before any path is calculated: “906.2 cm³ have to go. Target with T1 Flat end mill Ø 12: 33 min – on average ap 20.6 mm, 82 % of its metal removal rate.” It is calculated spot by spot: as many layers as the application's ap requires, each at its ae and feed – as if the cutter were never out of the cut. Where little has to go, the cutter only uses part of its ap: taking 5 mm off a face of 1000 × 1000 is a lot of volume, but the Ø 12 at ae 1.5 only reaches a fifth of its metal removal rate there (more than 12 hours); at 100 × 100 and 25 mm it is fully used. Every path takes longer than the target (air, plunging, rapids) – by how much tells how good it is. If another cutter from the tool manager would be clearly faster (by more than a fifth), it is named after it, with the smaller one that takes the rest in the corners afterwards: “Faster from the tool box: T2 Face mill Ø 50, then T1 Flat end mill Ø 12 for the rest – target 15 min.” Each cutter counts with its fastest application: a thin layer the Ø 12 with “Facing”, deep material with “Roughing”. Below it, Use the faster cutters: one click puts them into the strategies – a face mill into face milling and the one for the rest into clearing, a flat end mill into clearing and the one for the rest into rest machining –; then the competition is calculated again, and the target line counts with the cutter that is ticked. Optional: whoever wants to mill everything with one cutter leaves it. What the cutter cannot reach – corners and slots narrower than it – the sentence says too (“… remain for a smaller one”).

After the time of face milling and clearing it says how good their path is: “→ 1 layer, 163 rows, about 43 min · 1.41 × target” – their time divided by the target of their cutter with their values (ae, ap, feed) for what has to go down to their faces. Close to 1, the path is good; the larger the number, the more time goes into air, plunging and rapids.

5. Drilling

  • For through holes and blind holes with a drill tip, when the tool management has a drill with their diameter – the matching one is preselected. FreeCAD's “Hole” draws a blind hole with the tip of a drill (118°), the list then says “hole Ø 8.5, bottom 5, tip 118°”: a drill with this angle drills it, and its tip ends exactly where the model has it. A blind hole with a flat bottom a drill cannot make (its tip would remain); “Hole milling” mills it. A counterbore for a socket head screw is no through hole – it has a bottom, even though the through hole continues below.
  • Created is FreeCAD's Drilling with the cutting data of the application “Drilling”: the tip its length below the bottom so the hole has the full diameter all the way down; the R plane 3 mm above the stock; shallower than 3 × D in the material in one go (G81), deeper in pecks (G83) – depth per peck (empty: 1 × D each). Change it in FreeCAD's window (double-click).
  • Grey below: “→ 1 hole, 1 peck, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work (another diameter, flat bottom, a tip with another angle).
  • Different holes at once (a flange: the bolt circle Ø 9, the centre Ø 40): “Drilling” takes those its drill drills, “Hole milling” mills the rest – both ticked, “Create” creates both. Where both could do the same holes, the faster one wins.
  • A whole part at once (a mould plate: top, cavity, holes): every face gets its strategy – the top face milling or clearing, the holes drilling, the cavity 3D roughing and 3D finishing. A block that is red while another can do the same faces loses its tick; the red line says why.
  • Drilling, hole milling and contour solve the same task in a hole: the window computes all that work and ticks the fastest – with a matching drill usually drilling.

6. Hole milling

  • For holes: click the wall of a cylindrical hole – the list says “hole Ø 20, through” or “hole Ø 34, bottom 10”. An end mill smaller than the hole mills it completely; no drill needed, every size with one cutter.
  • Stepdown per layer (ap – only for holes larger than two cutters; smaller ones the helix mills in one go), row spacing (ae, this far the rings step outwards in large holes), allowance for finishing (empty: 0.3 mm) and the ticks finish the wall and climb milling (counter-clockwise in the hole, G3).
  • The path: down in a helix (G2/G3 with Z, as steep as the tool's plunge angle), once around at the bottom so the bottom is flat; in large holes rings outwards per layer, each with a half circle onto the next; finally the wall at the radius in one go, with half circles in from the middle and out again. Through holes 0.5 mm deeper so no burr remains. Several holes in the order of the shortest path.
  • Grey below: “→ 2 holes, 2 layers, 24 turns, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work (the hole is smaller than the cutter – then a smaller cutter or a drill).
  • Hole milling against contour: with only holes selected, the window computes both and ticks the faster (on the block with two holes: hole milling 1.3 min, the contour 4.6 – it clears every offset with its own ramp). With other walls selected too, the contour runs only those.
  • The operation is called “Bohrung fräsen T1”; the final depth is the lowest bottom (through holes 0.5 mm below).

7. Contour

  • For walls: vertical faces with a horizontal bottom edge – flat, round or free-form. The bottom edges of the selected walls join into contours: closed all round (around a boss, inside a pocket) or open (a single wall, a step). Where the material lies, the window knows from the wall itself.
  • Cutter and application as for face milling – “Roughing” preselected; the cutter radius is the distance of the path from the wall.
  • Stepdown per layer (ap) for roughing, row spacing (ae, sideways between the roughing passes, at most as wide as the cutter), allowance for finishing (empty: 0.3 mm – stays on the wall after roughing), material beside the wall (empty: as much as the stock says – outside everything to the edge, a pocket completely; after a pocket operation enter its allowance here) and the tick finishing in one go.
  • The path: roughing in layers from the top of the stock down to the bottom edge of the wall – per layer as many passes side by side as material stands beside the wall, from the outside towards the wall (last at radius plus allowance); then finishing once at the radius over the full height in one go (at most the cutting edge length per go). All in climb milling (spindle clockwise, M3): the material on the right of the direction of travel – around a boss clockwise, in a pocket counter-clockwise. Outer corners become arcs (G2/G3), inner ones keep the cutter radius. Every pass enters and leaves tangentially: a straight line away from the wall and a quarter circle (each the cutter radius) – where that does not fit, shorter, finally vertically. Before steps and walls that are not selected the path stops (computed against the part without the walls and their floors and tops). Where no stock lies, no pass runs; on leaving the stock at half feed. Down it goes vertically in the air with the plunge feed, in material over a ramp along the path.
  • Grey below: “→ 2 contours: 22 layers, 38 passes, about 9 min”. Red says why it does not work (no wall, the cutter does not fit into the pocket, nothing stands beside the walls). When clearing clears the pocket, its allowance (0.3) stands at “material beside the wall” by itself – then one pass remains, from the top edge of the pocket.
  • In the operation's properties also Deeper (this much deeper than the bottom edge – to cut free) and the lead-in radius.
  • The operation is called “Contour T1”; start depth, safe height and clearance height as for face milling, the final depth is the lowest bottom edge.

8. Tapping

  • For core holes, when the tool management has a tap whose core hole the hole has: thread Ø minus pitch, M10 × 1.5 → Ø 8.5. The matching one is preselected. The core hole is made before by “Drilling” or “Hole milling” – leave both ticked for the same hole.
  • You set the tick yourself: a thread is a decision, not a question of time – it competes with no strategy.
  • Created is FreeCAD's Tapping: G84, for a left-hand thread G74; the feed is pitch · speed. Through a through hole the tap goes its chamfer beyond (2 × pitch), in a blind hole it stays one pitch above the bottom. One operation per depth – FreeCAD runs all holes of an operation to its one final depth; the second is called “… (2)”. The same for drilling: one per bottom depth.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 × M10x1.5, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work (the core hole does not match, the tap has no pitch).
  • The operation is called “Thread M10x1.5 T3” – with a plain “x”: the name is written into the program as a comment, and not every control reads “×”.

9. Deburr

  • For top edges: click a wall – its top edge gets the chamfer – or a flat face on top – then all its edges where a wall goes down: the outer edges, the rims of pockets and holes; not the foot of a boss (there the wall goes up, there is no burr).
  • With a chamfer mill from the tool management; the application “Chamfering” is preselected. You set the tick yourself – which edge gets a chamfer is said by the drawing, not the model. Deburring comes last, when the edges are finished.
  • Chamfer width (empty: 0.3 mm) – this wide the chamfer becomes on top; with a 90° chamfer mill it reaches as far down the wall. Tip deeper (empty: 0.5 mm) – this much deeper than the chamfer stands the tip: the cone cuts the chamfer, not the tip, and the tip stays away from the wall (like FreeCAD's “Deburr”).
  • The path: one beside the edge, so far from the wall that the cone cuts the chamfer exactly as wide; climb milling (clockwise around a boss, counter-clockwise in a pocket), tangentially in and out, straight down in the air beside the wall. Outer corners become arcs. It stops before steps and walls not selected. If a wall is lower than the chamfer, its edge is left out – the line then says so.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 edge run, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work (no chamfer mill, the chamfer is wider than its cone cuts).
  • Modelled chamfers: if the model already has the chamfer (a sloped face at the top edge, as FreeCAD's “Chamfer” makes it), click the chamfer – or the face on top, then all chamfers at its edges come along. Width and angle come from the model, the field “Chamfer width” does not apply there; the cutter moves so that its cone lies exactly on the chamfer. The tick is proposed – the model says the chamfer comes. The chamfer mill needs its angle (a 45° chamfer: 90°); otherwise red says which one.
  • Rounding with a corner rounding mill (application “Rounding”): its tip stands exactly one radius below the edge, its axis half the pilot beside the wall – so it rounds a sharp edge with its radius. Modelled roundings (FreeCAD's “Fillet” at the top edge) the window recognises like chamfers; the rounding mill with their radius is preselected, and the line says “the rounding as modelled: R 2”. Its hollow must be a full quarter circle (pilot = Ø − 2 × radius) – otherwise a step would remain on top. Chamfer width and “tip deeper” do not apply to the rounding mill.
  • The operation is called “Deburr T3”. “Check on the machine” lets the chamfer pass: there it may go into the part as deep as the tip stands.

10. Spot drilling and countersinking

  • Spot drilling: for holes, with an NC spot drill from the tool management – before the drill comes. It goes so deep that a circle of Ø hole + 0.4 mm appears on top: the drill starts guided, and the edge gets a small chamfer at once (at most 0.9 × Ø of the spot drill). If the hole has a countersink on top, its top edge counts – while spotting it is still closed. You set the tick yourself.
  • Countersinking: for the countersinks the model has – click the conical face above a hole, the list says “countersink Ø 12.4, 90°”. A countersink tool with their angle and at least their Ø goes so deep that its cone has their Ø on top (its tip counts). The model says the countersink comes: the tick is proposed.
  • Both create FreeCAD's Drilling (G81), one operation per depth; spot drilling before drilling, countersinking after. Grey below: “→ 1 spot, 4.2 mm deep, about 1 min”.
  • “Check on the machine” computes drills, spot drills and countersink tools with their tip, not as cylinders; it lets the small chamfer of spot drilling pass.

11. Rest material

  • For walls, after a larger cutter: what it left in inner corners and at spots narrower than it, a smaller one takes – after the contour with Ø 12 a sharp corner keeps a radius 6, after the Ø 4 only a radius 2.
  • Cutter: the largest one smaller than the one before is preselected. Cutter before Ø (empty: the one of the contour, else the one of the clearing in this window) and Depth per layer (empty: from the application). The assistant sets the tick itself when the contour runs drawn inner fillets smaller than its cutter (a corner R 4 after the Ø 12) and a smaller one is there; otherwise you set it – and unticked by hand it stays off.
  • The path: one beside the wall, only where the small cutter's circle sticks out of the large one's way, and longer by its radius so it joins cleanly; in layers from the top of the wall down, climb milling, tangentially in and out, straight down in the open area the large one already cleared.
  • Grey below: “→ 4 spots, 2 layers, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work (the cutter before is not larger, it reached everywhere).
  • The operation is called “Rest material T3” – a contour with the property “RadiusDavor”; a double-click opens its block.

12. Thread milling

  • For core holes of metric threads, with a thread mill from the tool management – instead of “Tapping”: tick one and the other loses its tick (two threads in one hole would not work). Which thread, the core hole and the cutter's pitch say: Ø 8.5 with 1.5 → M10 × 1.5 (the table ISO 965, tolerance 6H, allows Ø 8.38 to 8.68 there). So one cutter mills every thread of its pitch it fits into. You set the tick yourself.
  • It mills to the middle of the tolerance of the pitch diameter (M10 × 1.5: 9.116). If the thread is too tight or too loose, the operation's property Korrektur changes the diameter – after the gauge: if the go side does not enter, a little more.
  • The path per hole: rapid down in the middle, in on a half circle (a quarter pitch in z on the way – as steep as the thread, no step), the helix with one pitch per turn, back to the middle on a half circle. Climb milling (the material on the right, M3) goes counter-clockwise, a right-hand thread thus from the bottom up; unticked, conventional milling from the top down. Left-hand threads: property “Linksgewinde”.
  • The feed applies at the cutting edge: in the hole the edge travels much further than the centre of the cutter (M10 with Ø 8: almost five times). So the centre moves correspondingly slower – with the application's feed at the centre the cutter would break. FreeCAD's own thread milling does not do this.
  • Through a through hole it goes so far beyond that the lowest turn is complete (Ø 8 for M10 × 1.5: about 1.3 mm); at the top the turn ends completely above the edge. In a blind hole the tip stays 0.2 mm above the bottom – the thread reaches almost to the bottom, unlike with a tap.
  • A cutter with several teeth stacked (cutting length 15 mm at pitch 1.5: ten) mills that many turns at once and needs only as many revolutions as it takes for its top tooth to reach the top – usually one. A cutter with one tooth (cutting length below two pitches) mills turn by turn. If the cutting length in the tool management is wrong, the thread is wrong – the operation's property “Zaehne” shows what it calculates with.
  • Grey below: “→ 2 × M10x1.5, 2 turns, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work: the hole is no core hole for the pitch (with the core hole it would need), the cutter is too large, its neck too thick, it does not reach deep enough.
  • The operation is called “Thread milling T7”. “Check on the machine” lets the thread pass: around each hole it may go into the part up to the tip of the tooth, not beside it.

19. Rest roughing

  • After 3D roughing, rough with a smaller cutter (flat face) only what the large one left – in narrow gaps between freeform faces, in corners and fillets it could not get down into. You set the tick yourself; preselected is the largest cutter smaller than the one of 3D roughing.
  • Cutter before Ø (empty: the one of 3D roughing in the window, with its shape): what remains is computed from its envelope – as deep as its flat face got. Entered by hand, the cutter before counts as a flat end mill. The other fields as in 3D roughing.
  • There it runs layers like 3D roughing (full depth of cut, intermediate layers for the steps), but only where material stands above the layer that the small one reaches. The steps the large one left on the faces remain for finishing.
  • If there is no rest anywhere, it says so in red. The operation is called “Rest roughing T5” and comes after 3D roughing.

18. Rest finishing

  • After 3D finishing, finish again with a smaller ball end mill (or bull nose) only where the large one could not reach – in fillets, tight curves and corners of the freeform faces. You set the tick yourself; preselected is the largest ball end mill smaller than the one of 3D finishing.
  • Cutter before Ø (empty: the one of 3D finishing in the window, with its shape): from it, the surface the large one left is computed against the one the small one reaches – where the large one left more than 0.01 mm, there is rest. Entered by hand, the cutter before counts as a ball end mill. Scallop height and stock as in 3D finishing.
  • There it runs like 3D finishing: contour lines where it is steep, rows or a spiral elsewhere – the fastest counts; by the radius of the small cutter and one row spacing beyond the rest, so that nothing remains.
  • Grey below: “→ 10 contours and a spiral with …, about 4 min” (dome Ø 40 on the plate, ball Ø 2 after ball Ø 6 – the whole dome with Ø 2 would need 9 min). In the fillet at the foot 0.18 instead of 0.44 mm remain. If there is no rest anywhere, it says so in red. The operation is called “Rest finishing T4”.

17. Pencil

  • For the fillets at freeform faces: where the ball end mill touches the selected face and its neighbour at once – at the foot of a dome on the plate, in a groove. The rows of 3D finishing end there; between their ends a rest remains, which the pencil pass takes in one go.
  • You set the tick yourself (click the face, tick “Pencil”); the ball end mill of 3D finishing is preselected. Allowance (empty: 0): this much stays in the fillet.
  • The fillet is found on the cutter's envelope: where it kinks upwards (the slope jumps by more than 17°), the cutter touches two faces. The line lies exactly there, not on the raster; the height is computed at every point.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 fillet, 135 mm long, about 1 min” (the dome Ø 40 with a ball Ø 6: the ring at r 21.45 around the centre). If there is no fillet, it says so in red. The operation is called “Pencil T3” and comes after 3D finishing.

16. 3D roughing

  • For freeform faces as with 3D finishing: click them, and next to “3D finishing” “3D roughing” is ticked too – with the cutter for clearing (end mill or bull-nose, flat tip). It clears the stock above the faces before the ball finishes.
  • The path: layers from the top down to the lowest point of the faces plus allowance, each at full step-down with a narrow ae – the rings as in Clearing, climb milling. Where the envelope of the part lies above the layer, the cutter stays out: in every layer the dome is an island with allowance.
  • Above a freeform face each full layer would leave a step as high as the step-down. So it then runs intermediate layers (empty: 1 mm apart) up to the previous layer – only where material still stands above the face, from the outside up to the face. As an intermediate layer is thin, its rings may lie further apart (as much material per mm as in the full layer, at most half the diameter). 0 means no intermediate layers.
  • What remains is the allowance (also on the floor below) and the steps: on the dome 0.4 to 1.6 mm normal to the face. In a hollow (bowl) more remains under the flat tip – the arc under the cutter.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 layer and 9 intermediate layers, 26 rings, about 5 min” (standard cutter, the dome Ø 40 on the plate 60 × 60). In a bowl the full layer finds nothing, then only the intermediate layers count. The operation is called “3D roughing T1”.

15. 3D finishing

  • For freeform faces – domes, fillets, slopes: faces facing up but neither flat nor vertical. Click them, the list says “freeform, bottom 10”; the tick is set, a ball end mill is preselected (bull-nose and end mills work too).
  • The path: parallel rows, the tip on the envelope of the whole part – the cutter touches the selected faces and never cuts into the part, not into the faces beside them either. It mills only where the selected faces set the height of the cutter: at the foot of a dome the row ends where the cutter touches the plate beside it; the plate stays as it is.
  • Scallop height (empty: 0.01 mm): at most this high the ridges between two rows remain – the row spacing follows (ball Ø 6: 0.49 mm). On steep spots the rows lie further apart in space and the ridges grow there – the limit of rows. Allowance: this much remains, normal to the face.
  • Steep/shallow: where the face is steeper than 45°, rows would lie far apart in space – there it runs contours: the tip at a fixed height around the face, from top to bottom, as far apart in z as the rows in the plane, climb milling (material on the right). The rows run only where it is shallower; at the border both overlap. On a hemisphere the flank keeps 0.026 mm instead of 0.056 with rows alone. The operation's property “Grenzwinkel” changes the border, 0 means rows only.
  • Computed along X, along Y, as a spiral and along the face, the fastest counts. Rows zigzag row by row, between near ends it glides across 0.1 mm above the face, otherwise it lifts. The spiral runs from the centre outwards, one row spacing further per turn, without reversals (climb milling) – on a dome 12 % faster than rows. Along the face (flowline) the paths follow the curves of the face itself – on a dome the circles of latitude, on a cylinder the lines along its axis –, one row spacing apart in space everywhere, shallow or steep: no contours needed, closed circles in climb milling. On the dome another 13 % faster than the spiral, on a long cylinder 30 % faster than rows. Equidistant lays rings from the edge of the selected faces inwards, one row spacing apart in space everywhere – across all selected faces, shallow or steep; where the rings of both sides end at a ridge (the middle of a rectangle) it runs along the ridge once. It leaves the least (on the dome 0.017 instead of 0.020 mm) but takes a little longer there. Spiral, along the face and equidistant compete only with steep/shallow (otherwise it computes rows only); the operation's property “Richtung” fixes one. Few blocks on straight stretches, in curves as many as the accuracy needs.
  • It mills only where the cutter touches the selected faces – not in the margin where it merely rolls over the outer edge of a part or a step: there it removes nothing, and contours and spiral used to cut up to 0.05 mm into the side there.
  • Grey below: “→ 46 curves along the face, spacing 0.49, about 4 min”, “→ 51 rings equidistant, …”, “→ 6 contours and a spiral with 45 turns, …” or “→ 87 rows along X, …”. Rough first – “3D roughing” is ticked along with it (section 16) –, otherwise the ball cuts into solid material. The operation is called “3D finishing T3”.

14. Reaming

  • For holes that must be to size (H7): with a reamer from the tool management that has the diameter of the hole – the matching one is preselected. You set the tick yourself: whether a hole is reamed is not in the model.
  • With the tick “Drilling” pre-drills smaller: a drill 0.15 to 0.5 mm undersize (for Ø 10 one of Ø 9.5 to 9.85 – the largest is preselected), the line says “… – pre-drilled for reaming, with Ø 9.8”. “Hole milling” and the contour do not compete in these holes – they would mill to size, leaving the reamer nothing to do.
  • Created is FreeCAD's Drilling with G85: in at feed and out again at feed (pulled out at rapid the reamer would leave marks). Through holes 1 mm below the bottom, blind holes with a drill tip to the bottom of the wall; no drill can pre-drill a blind hole with a flat bottom – reaming does not work there. The operation is called “Reaming T4” and comes after drilling and countersinking.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 hole, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work (no reamer with this diameter, flat bottom).

13. Slot

  • For slots – two straight walls with semicircles at the ends: click a wall or the floor, the list says “slot 20 × 50, bottom 10” or “…, through”; one wall means the whole slot. With an end mill no wider than the slot – fastest at 50 to 75 % of its width; one that fits is preselected.
  • The path never cuts at full width with the whole flute. If the slot is wider than the cutter: down in a helix at one end, once all round at the bottom, then in arcs up to the semicircle at the other end – each arc a semicircle, climb milling from one wall forward through the material to the other (a dent forms in the middle), back across the free side at high feed (3 × vf) and a little forward along the wall into the next; in layers of ap, the next layer back. How far the arcs advance follows the load: per arc the cutter removes on average as much material per mm of path as on a straight path with ae. On every arc the engagement grows from the wall to the middle and falls again – in the middle briefly up to 1.7 ae, above 1.25 ae never for longer than one cutter width of path, and never more than half the cutter engaged. In the slot 20 wide with the Ø 12 and ae 1.5 that is 0.97 mm per arc. If it is barely wider (the arcs would have a radius under a quarter of the cutter radius): down in a zigzag ramp along the slot, each pass at most so deep that at full width it never cuts more than ap and never more than half the diameter – with the feed lowered so the chip stays as thick as with the application's small ae.
  • Finally the wall once all round climb milling (counter-clockwise, G3), with semicircles in from the centre of one end and out again, in passes of at most the flute length. Through slots 0.5 mm deeper.
  • Fields as for “Hole milling”: stepdown per layer (ap of the arcs), row spacing (ae – it gives the step of the arcs), allowance for finishing, the ticks finish the wall and climb milling. If the cutter mills the slot at full width, the application “Full slot” is preselected when it has one.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 slot, 1 layer, 31 arcs, about 1 min” or “→ 1 slot at full width with a ramp, about 1 min”. Red says why it does not work: the cutter is wider than the slot, or a closed slot is wider than nearly two cutters – a core would stay in the middle of the helix (a larger cutter, or “Clearing”).
  • Open slots: open towards the edge – at one end (a semicircle and two walls ending at the edge) or at both (two parallel walls with the floor between). The list says “open slot 16 × 60, floor 12”. Behind the open end there is air: there the cutter goes down and enters from outside – no helix, no plunge. Then it mills in arcs: each arc a semicircle, climb milling from one wall forward through the material to the other – a dent forms in the middle –, back across the free side at high feed (3 × vf), and a little forward along the wall into the next arc. The first arcs blend gradually from the straight edge into the semicircle. How far the arcs advance follows the load as above: in a narrow slot every arc sweeps the whole width on a short path – the step is smaller than ae (in the slot 16 with the Ø 12 0.58 mm), in wide ones somewhat larger. Every layer starts outside, so an open slot may be any width. It finishes the walls climb milling: in along one wall, across outside (or round the semicircle), out along the other. Plate 60 × 40, slot 16 wide, 8 deep, all the way through: 1.25 min (the contour at the walls 2.2). The line says “→ 1 slot, 1 layer, 118 arcs, about …”.
  • Time decides: at the walls the slot competes with the contour (in the slot 20 × 50, 10 deep, the slot is ahead – the contour's line says “28 % slower than Slot”; in the open slot 16 × 60 it is 21 %). On the floor clearing and face milling compute as well but do not compete: in the slot they would first cut full width – more than ae, only seemingly faster (at the open slot 0.19 min, three times faster than ae · ap allows, and with a rapid into material). Their line says so.
  • The operation is called “Slot T1”; the final depth is the deepest floor (through: 0.5 mm below).

Changing it later

Double-click “Face milling …”, “Clearing …”, “Slot …”, “Hole milling …”, “Contour …”, “Deburr …” or “Thread milling …” in the tree – or right-click, Edit; or select the operation or its job and press the “Machining (milling)” button – to open the window with faces, cutter, application and values of the operation, with its block only. Apply changes it – one undo step; another cutter gets its own tool controller, the old one is removed if no operation uses it any more.
