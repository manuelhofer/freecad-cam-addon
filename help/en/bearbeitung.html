Machining (milling)

For a part that lies in a block on a mill – the tool axis is vertical. The button “Machining (milling)” opens the window; the way from the part to the operations has three steps, all in one window: part and stock, the faces, then one block with a tick per machining – Face milling and Clearing for flat faces facing up, Contour for walls. “Create” creates all ticked ones. For a round stock with a rotary axis there is 4-axis machining.

1. Part and stock

  • Click a face of the part (before or in the window) – the face that should become flat, or a wall for the contour. The part is chosen with it, and the job with the stock is created at once: a block around the part with an allowance on top, at the sides and below (empty: 1 mm, like FreeCAD's job). The fields change the stock right away. “Cancel” takes everything back.
  • Zero point – where X 0, Y 0, Z 0 of the program lie: “as in the model”, or a point of the stock from the list – the 8 corners, the 12 edge middles, the centre top and bottom (left/right is X, front/back Y, bottom/top Z) –, shifted by X, Y, Z (negative too). Part and stock move so that the point lies at the origin; the axes in the 3D view show it, and “Check on the machine” counts from there.
  • When changing (double-click on the operation) stock and zero point stay as they are in the job.

2. Faces

  • Without a choice the top of the part is milled flat – the highest flat faces. The sentence below the list names them.
  • A click on a face of the part adds it (another click, or a double-click in the list, removes it); green means: flat facing up – it is milled flat – or a wall (“wall, bottom 5 mm”) – the contour follows it; red means neither. The 3D view colours them so. “Top” selects the highest faces, “Clear selection” removes all.
  • The ticks of the blocks follow the selection: face milling and clearing as long as no face or a flat one is selected – and of the two only the faster (see “Time decides” below); contour as soon as a wall is among them (without a wall it cannot be ticked). Whoever sets or removes a tick keeps it.

3. Face milling

  • Material – from the stock, otherwise the last used; the cutting data follow it. “Tool manager …” opens it; saving there makes the window read the cutters again.
  • Cutter – a cutter with a flat tip (flat, torus or face mill) from the tool manager with cutting data for the material; Application – “Facing” preselected; speed and feed in grey below.
  • Stepdown per layer (ap), row spacing (ae, at most as wide as the flat part of the tip) and allowance – empty, the grey suggestions from the application apply.
  • The path: rows along X or Y – the assistant computes both directions with their time and takes the faster one; the result line says how much slower the other would be –, back and forth, in layers from the top of the stock down to the face plus allowance; along the rows the cutter centre runs 0.6 × diameter past the face, at the sides the tip reaches 0.2 × diameter past the edge – so no burr remains. Between the rows it runs semicircles (G2/G3) where both rows are free there, otherwise across at depth. Before walls and steps that stand higher the row stops (calculated against the part without the face). Rows with no stock below them are dropped. Before a wall the cutter runs along it – back to the previous row and past the first and last row to the edge of the face – so that nothing remains between the rows and in the corners at the wall. Entry is straight down with the plunge feed in the air, a ramp in material. Leaving the stock the row runs at half feed – against the burr. What it looks like shows Check on machine: the block is removed while playing, at the end in colours against the finished part.
  • In grey below: “→ 3 layers, 30 rows, about 2 min” – the time with the feed, rapid 10 m/min and acceleration 1 m/s² (fixed defaults, see Finding the acceleration), stopping at every corner, so that strategies compare. In red stands why it does not work (no cutter with cutting data, no flat face facing up, the stock does not reach above the face).
  • Create adds the tool controller and the operation “Face milling T1” to the job – one undo step (job and stock a second one). The operation has FreeCAD's depths and heights: the start depth follows the stock, the final depth is the face plus allowance, safe and clearance height come from the job's setup sheet.

4. Clearing

  • For the same flat faces as face milling – and for pocket floors: when the walls of a pocket are selected, clearing takes their floor along (it is not ticked by itself for that – the contour stays the proposal; set the tick when the pocket is to be cleared).
  • Cutter and application as for face milling – “Roughing” preselected.
  • Stepdown per layer (ap – full, up to the cutting edge length), row spacing (ae, this far the rings step, at most the radius), allowance at walls (empty: 0.3 mm – stays on walls and islands, for the contour), allowance at the floor (empty: 0 – the face is finished) and the tick climb milling (material on the left of the direction of travel; without it conventional).
  • The path, like an HSM path: rings at full stepdown and narrow row spacing, without turning, with arcs in the corners. On a top face circling from the outside inwards: the first ring lies beside the stock in the air, the cutter plunges there and enters tangentially; the rings follow the stock edge inwards until bosses and steps split them – then the rings around those. In a pocket from the inside outwards: once over a ramp all round (with the tool's plunge angle) into the middle, then the rings towards the wall. Every ring joins the previous one (a spiral); where that is not possible, the cutter plunges only where it is already free, otherwise it enters sideways from the free area – the ramp in material exists only where nothing is free. On leaving the stock at half feed.
  • The window computes three variants and takes the fastest: “rohteil” (the rings around what still stands: from the stock edge and from the islands, each ae further, equally spaced everywhere – the rectangle bites into the boss, the rings around it come last), “morph” (rectangle rings until one would hit the boss, then they get rounder from ring to ring until the last one only circles the boss – one spiral without lifting, rings closer where the gap is narrow) and “inseln” (rings only around the walls and islands). In the operation's properties Gerechnet lists each with its time; Variante fixes one.
  • Grey below: “→ 1 layer, 15 rings, about 3 min”. Red says why it does not work (no flat floor, the cutter does not fit in, nothing stands above the face).
  • The operation is called “Räumen T1”; start depth, safe height and clearance height as for face milling, the final depth is the lowest face plus the allowance at the floor.

Time decides

Face milling and clearing solve the same task on a flat face. So the window computes both as soon as one is ticked, with the same time model (feed of the application, rapid 10 m/min, 1 m/s², stopping at corners) and ticks the faster; both lines say by how much: “… – the fastest; clearing would be 37 % slower” and “… – 37 % slower than face milling”. On the 60 × 40 block with a step face milling wins, on the 50 × 50 with a boss in the middle clearing does (3 instead of 5 minutes), on a large plate likewise. Whoever sets a tick keeps it – the lines still show the comparison. For a pocket the fastest sequence is: clearing (its floor, the walls with allowance) and then the contour with “material beside the wall” equal to the allowance – the contour alone clears every offset with its own ramp and takes several times as long.

5. Contour

  • For walls: vertical faces with a horizontal bottom edge – flat, round or free-form. The bottom edges of the selected walls join into contours: closed all round (around a boss, inside a pocket) or open (a single wall, a step). Where the material lies, the window knows from the wall itself.
  • Cutter and application as for face milling – “Roughing” preselected; the cutter radius is the distance of the path from the wall.
  • Stepdown per layer (ap) for roughing, row spacing (ae, sideways between the roughing passes, at most as wide as the cutter), allowance for finishing (empty: 0.3 mm – stays on the wall after roughing), material beside the wall (empty: as much as the stock says – outside everything to the edge, a pocket completely; after a pocket operation enter its allowance here) and the tick finishing in one go.
  • The path: roughing in layers from the top of the stock down to the bottom edge of the wall – per layer as many passes side by side as material stands beside the wall, from the outside towards the wall (last at radius plus allowance); then finishing once at the radius over the full height in one go (at most the cutting edge length per go). All in climb milling: the material on the left of the direction of travel – around a boss counter-clockwise, in a pocket clockwise. Outer corners become arcs (G2/G3), inner ones keep the cutter radius. Every pass enters and leaves tangentially: a straight line away from the wall and a quarter circle (each the cutter radius) – where that does not fit, shorter, finally vertically. Before steps and walls that are not selected the path stops (computed against the part without the walls and their floors and tops). Where no stock lies, no pass runs; on leaving the stock at half feed. Down it goes vertically in the air with the plunge feed, in material over a ramp along the path.
  • Grey below: “→ 2 contours: 22 layers, 38 passes, about 9 min”. Red says why it does not work (no wall, the cutter does not fit into the pocket, nothing stands beside the walls). After clearing a pocket enter its allowance (0.3) at “material beside the wall” – then one pass per layer and the finishing remain.
  • In the operation's properties also Deeper (this much deeper than the bottom edge – to cut free) and the lead-in radius.
  • The operation is called “Contour T1”; start depth, safe height and clearance height as for face milling, the final depth is the lowest bottom edge.

Changing it later

Double-click “Face milling …”, “Clearing …” or “Contour …” in the tree – or right-click, Edit; or select the operation or its job and press the “Machining (milling)” button – to open the window with faces, cutter, application and values of the operation, with its block only. Apply changes it – one undo step; another cutter gets its own tool controller, the old one is removed if no operation uses it any more.
