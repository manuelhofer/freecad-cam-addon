Swivel plane (3+2)

On a 5-axis mill the part stays clamped; the rotary axes swivel the plane until the tool stands perpendicular on an inclined face. There the machine mills like a 3-axis machine – facing, clearing, contour, drilling. In the addon every swivelled plane becomes a job of its own: in it the face points up, and every strategy computes as always.

How to

  1. The part has a job as it is clamped on the table – the base job (the “Machining” assistant creates it; if it is missing, “Swivel plane” says so and opens the assistant at the press of a button). Best with the 5-axis machine set on the job (step 1 of the assistant or “Check on machine”).
  2. Click the inclined face on the part and Swivel plane (3+2) … in the CAM-Addon toolbar – or select the inclined face in the Machining assistant, where the button Swivel plane (3+2) … appears. For an angled hole with no flat face around its entry (in a fillet, say), click its wall: the tool then stands in the hole's axis, the plane goes through its open end. With no suitable face: Or angles – tilt and direction (“15° towards 90°”: 15° from the vertical, the head towards +Y), then Apply. 3D finishing, say, then mills with a tilted ball end mill: the same surface in the same time, only the ball no longer cuts with its tip on top (where the cutting speed is 0) – at 15° with Ø 1.55 instead of 0 (ball Ø 6).
  3. Under Tool in the job, choose the tool whose direction should be checked. Its T number selects the mount (the turret slot); its length and angled holder determine the actual tool axis. Changing the tool checks the direction again. The window shows the base job, the machine and in green how far the plane is swivelled and where the rotary axes go: “Face12: swivelled 30° → A−30 C0”. Clicking another face while it is open takes that one. Red says why it does not work (neither flat nor the wall of a hole, the loaded rotary axes cannot get there).
  4. OK creates the plane's job (“Job … – plane Face12 (A−30 C0)”): the model rotated, the base job's stock rotated with it. With the tick the Machining assistant opens in it right away, the face selected – choose strategies and create as always. If the plane exists already (the same face, or a parallel one), the window says so, and OK opens it instead of creating a second one.
  5. Write program for the base job: its planes go into the program, each swivelled once – with Siemens by CYCLE800, with Heidenhain by PLANE SPATIAL, otherwise computed from the selected machine (Write program). Check on machine for the base job runs the planes too, with the retract and swivel in between; the player shows “… – plane A−30 C0”. Base job and planes share one tool loading.

3 or 4 axes: A fixed spindle direction or a single positioning rotary axis is also read from the actual machine. Unreachable directions remain disabled; OK is enabled only for a permissible direction. A matching horizontal face uses the base job. A different CAM frame without rotary axes writes only transformed XYZ motions, with no imaginary swivel cycle. Changes to stops, the job machine, tool or holder are checked again before OK. If stops change after creating the jobs, planes that are no longer reachable are explicitly omitted as unmachined from the program. Their unperformed cuts remain in spatial stock; reachable planes remain in the program. Without a job tool, OK remains disabled. Subsequent machining selects its tools as usual; each operation is checked with its own tool setup.

How it is computed

  • The plane: Z is the face normal out of the material – the tool axis; X is the base job's X laid into the plane; the origin is the point of the plane closest to the base job's zero point.
  • The rotary axes: from the machine's chain – the position in which the tool stands perpendicular on the face relative to the workpiece, within the limits, with the smallest swivel. The machine assigned to the job remains binding even if another 5-axis machine is open. Its loaded limits are shown in the window. Without a readable assignment, no imaginary machine is assumed.
  • The zero point of the plane on the machine is the base job's: the machine sees the part as it is clamped.

What is already gone: a plane starts where material is left after the base job and the planes before it (higher in the tree) – like every operation with material state. If the base job changes, the plane's operations recompute.

A preceding simultaneous operation also contributes its actual output axis motions. The selected machine determines the spatial movement of the ball mill or finite cylindrical cutting section during swivelling. Omitted cutting strokes leave their material in place. Unknown cutter shapes, uncertain motion or excessive computation fall back conservatively to the original stock. This material state is a raster preview; precise finished-part and collision checks remain necessary.

Simultaneous

A swivelled plane stays fixed while milling. Where the tool axis should change from point to point – the ball mill tilted, tilting away in a cavity, the flank on an inclined wall –, use the Machining wizard (3D finishing, flank), 5-axis simultaneous. A fixed lead angle also works here: “Or angles” (above).
