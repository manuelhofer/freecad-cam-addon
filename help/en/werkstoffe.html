Materials

At the top of the tool manager you choose the material being cut. It comes first because a tool's cutting data depends on it: the same end mill runs quite differently in aluminium and in stainless steel.

How a material is named

The way it is known in the workshop:

1.4301  X5CrNi18-10 · Stainless steel, austenitic (V2A, AISI 304)

  • 1.4301 – the European material number.
  • X5CrNi18-10 – the short name from the chemical composition: high alloy (X), 0.05 % carbon, 18 % chromium, 10 % nickel.
  • Stainless steel, austenitic – the group, for some materials with the condition (annealed, quenched and tempered, hardened, T6 …). “1.2379 soft annealed” and “1.2379 hardened” are two entries because they need entirely different cutting data.
  • In brackets: old or common names such as V2A, GG-25, 304, EN AW-6082 …

Below the selection you see the composition (mass %), the hardness, the tensile strength and the ISO group.

Searching

Just type into the field – anything that appears in the line is found: “1.43”, “X5Cr”, “304”, “stainless”, “GG-25”, “6082”.

The ISO groups

The coloured box is the letter used in tool catalogues. Manufacturers sort their cutting data by it – that is where you find the values for your tool.

 P steel – including ferritic and martensitic stainless steel
 M stainless steel, austenitic and duplex
 K cast iron
 N aluminium, copper, brass, bronze, plastics
 S titanium, nickel and superalloys
 H hardened steel

All materials

The first entry of the selection. What a tool has there applies to every material for which it has no values of its own.

Where the data comes from

Composition, hardness and tensile strength are typical values from the standards (EN). The inspection certificate of your material is what counts.
