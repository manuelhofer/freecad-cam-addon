Holders

The holder sits between spindle nose and tool: collet chuck, shrink-fit chuck, Weldon holder, drill chuck … It is what bumps into things – in deep pockets on the part, close to the vise, on a jaw. That is why the collision check (“Check on machine”) knows it, the run-through shows it, and as long as nobody has measured the gauge length, the tool manager estimates it with the holder.

How to

In the tool manager, next to the tool's gauge length: Holder – the selection, and Holders … opens the window with all holders. There New → empty or a template. With OK the tool gets the chosen holder. Saving happens with OK or Apply of the tool manager; Cancel discards everything.

A holder

  • Name – how it is called in the selection, e.g. “SK40 ER32 A70”. Description – free text: manufacturer, order number.
  • Clamping depth – how deep the shank sits in the holder. The tool manager uses it to estimate the gauge length: holder length + overall length of the tool − clamping depth, at least until the cutting part and neck are free. Better: enter with the tool how far it sticks out of the holder (“stick-out” – in a collet each one sits at a different depth), or the measured gauge length (presetter); then the clamping depth does not count.
  • Contour from the spindle nose – section by section from the spindle nose (the flange face) towards the tool: each with length, Ø top (towards the spindle) and Ø bottom (towards the tool). Equal diameters make a cylinder, different ones a cone. What sits inside the spindle – steep taper, HSK shank – does not count. From the catalogue: flange diameter and thickness, then the body down to the nose; the whole length is usually called A or L1 there. The flange and the collet nut of the templates are one row each; drawn (and in “Check collision”) the flange has its gripper groove, the nut its chamfers – the row's tooltip says so. Groove and chamfers follow when you change their dimensions.
  • + Section inserts one below the chosen one, − Section removes it.
  • Direction – how the tool stands to the machine. straight: in the axis of the mount, as in a milling spindle or an axial VDI holder. angled: a driven radial holder on the turret or an angle head in the milling spindle. Then there are four more fields: the angle between the axis of the mount and that of the tool (90° = right angle), the rotation about the axis of the mount (0°: towards its reference direction – on the turret towards the spindle axis), the offset from the mount to the tool axis and the head Ø for the head down to there. Where the two axes meet is the reference point: the contour runs from it along the tool axis, and with the tool the length is then called length from reference point – as a presetter measures driven tools. The check window, the run-through and the collision check place the tool that way.
  • On the right the picture: the holder in section, the spindle on top, below it the tool down to its tip – shank grey, cutting part blue as in the tool's picture; an angled holder with its head and bend (drawn in the plane of the bend – the rotation does not show). You see at once whether the dimensions fit together.
  • Below, which tools use the holder. A holder is a description, not a single piece – several tools may share one.

Templates

To get started there are templates: collet chucks ER16 to ER40, shrink-fit, Weldon, hydraulic chuck, shell mill arbor, drill chuck and a 90° angle head – on the tool interface of the open machine (entered on the spindle, “New machine” or the spindle's values; none: SK40). The flange is the interface's, the body below that of the clamping system; the name says it (“ER32 collet chuck · HSK-A63”). Under For another interface are the same templates for every interface: SK30/40/50 (DIN 69871), BT30/40/50 (MAS 403), CAT40/50, HSK-A32 … A100, HSK-E32 … E50, HSK-F63 (DIN 69893), Capto C3 … C8 (ISO 26623) and KM32 … KM80. And VDI holders for lathes: axial, driven axial and driven radial – the driven ones each with ER16, ER20, ER25 or ER32 (“New” → “VDI driven radial” → “ER25”). They name no size: if a machine is open that knows its VDI size (turret, “New machine”), their diameters follow it and the name says so (“VDI40 …”); otherwise the dimensions are those for VDI 30. The collet nut keeps its size – it depends on the collet, not on the mount.

The templates at the spindle are rebuilt from the catalogue drawings of real holders (Hoffmann; the description names the article): the flange with its gripper groove as SK40, BT40 and HSK-A63 have it, then body, neck and collet nut. The Link field leads to the model in the catalogue – on an interface other than SK40 to the search for this kind of holder on it. Open shows the page in the browser. Your own holder differs: enter its dimensions from the catalogue and its link. The VDI holders and the angle head still have typical values; the description says so.

New → From file (STEP, STL) … reads your own holder's CAD file (e.g. from the manufacturer): the add-on finds the axis of rotation and the side with the taper. From the taper's end, the spindle nose, it measures the contour to the nose, as sections to 0.1 mm. Then enter the clamping depth and compare the length with the A dimension in the catalogue. If it finds no taper, it says so – then the contour starts at one end of the file and you delete the sections up to the spindle nose. The file stays where it is.

Limits

  • The holder is round about its axes – an angled one with the head about the axis of the mount and the output about the tool axis. Block holders on lathes (VDI) are approximated by cylinders.
  • The holder is not handed to CAM – FreeCAD's CAM tools do not know holders.
