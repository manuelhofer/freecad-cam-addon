Plan roughing values

The question behind it: How do I remove the most material with this cutter in this material? The answer of dynamic milling is: use the full flute length, step over narrowly and raise the feed until each tooth still cuts a proper chip. The planner works this out for a series of widths, checks each against the limits of tool and machine and suggests the one with the highest removal rate.

Why fz rises when ae gets smaller

In a full slot each tooth is in the material for half a revolution, and the chip in the middle is as thick as fz. With a narrow step-over each tooth only brushes the material – and the chip gets thinner than fz. At ae = 10 % of D it is only 60 % of fz. Chips that are too thin don't cut, they rub: the edge gets hot and dull without much happening.

So instead of fz you set the chip thickness h each tooth should cut, and fz is worked out from it (chip thinning compensation):

fz = h / sin φ, with the engagement angle φ from cos φ = 1 − 2 · ae / D (from ae = D/2 on, fz = h)

The fields

  • Cutting speed vc – from the manufacturer's catalogue. Many manufacturers allow more with a small engagement than for slotting, because the edge cools down between two cuts.
  • Chip thickness h – taken from the selected row of the table (for a full slot it is fz). For dynamic milling manufacturers often state a little more than for slotting.
  • Depth ap – how much of the flute length cuts. The default is the flute length, at most 2 × D. More ap means: the wear spreads over more edge.
  • ae at most – how wide the cutter may step over at full flute length. Manufacturers usually state 5 to 15 % of D – more in aluminium, less in stainless steel, titanium and hardened steel. The default is 10 %.
  • Machine – maximum speed, maximum cutting feed and spindle power. An empty field means: no limit. If the machine is described with Edit machine and its document is open, From the machine takes speed and feed from it; if it is exactly one and both fields are empty, its values are already filled in. The planner remembers the values for next time.

The table

One row per width, from 2 % to 50 % of D: the compensated fz, the feed vf, the removal rate Q and – if the material has a kc1.1 value – the cutting power at the edge. The spindle speed is shown above; it is the same for all rows.

  • Green is the suggestion: the row with the highest Q that keeps all limits. The sentence below says why it can't be wider.
  • Grey rows are beyond a limit – wider than allowed or more power than the spindle has.
  • Feed at its limit: with a very narrow ae, fz gets large; if the machine can't reach the feed, the planner uses its maximum feed – the chip then gets thinner than wanted.
  • Spindle at full power: if the power limit lies between two rows, the planner finds the ae right at it (to 0.01 mm) and adds it as a row of its own.

Click another row and Add as use case takes that one instead of the suggestion. The values go into the table as Dynamic roughing, rounded down: ae to 0.01 mm, fz to 0.001 mm. If the selected material has no values of its own yet, the button creates them for it.

For comparison: the full slot with fz 0.05 mm and ap 3 mm removes 17.2 cm³/min. With the same chip thickness the narrow, deep row removes a third more – and spares the edge: 24 instead of 3 mm of edge are cutting, and each tooth cools down for 90 % of the revolution. That is why manufacturers usually allow a larger chip thickness and a higher vc for dynamic milling. With h = 0.09 mm – at ae 1.2 mm that is fz 0.15 mm – it is 41.2 cm³/min, 2.4 times the full slot.

What the planner doesn't know

  • The limit for ae stands for what the tool can take – bending, vibration, chip space. The planner doesn't calculate that, which is why it is a field.
  • The power is rough (kc1.1 model), and many spindles only deliver their full power above a certain speed. The torque at low speed is not checked.
  • Dynamic milling needs a rigid setup, a short stick-out and a machine that handles the many changes of direction. In FreeCAD CAM it is called Adaptive: step over = ae, step down = ap.
